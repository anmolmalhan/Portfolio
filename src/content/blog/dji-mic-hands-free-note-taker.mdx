Meeting note takers mostly work by joining your call as a bot. Everyone sees it
in the participant list, your audio goes to somebody's server, and you get a
summary back. That is a lot of moving parts for "write down what was said".

You can do the whole thing on your own machine instead. A DJI wireless mic
handles the audio, [MacParakeet](https://macparakeet.com) transcribes it locally
on Apple Silicon, and the transcript never leaves your Mac.

There is also a trick hiding in the hardware. The link button on the DJI
transmitter already sends a real keypress to your Mac over USB, which means the
mic clipped to your shirt can start and stop the recording. That part is the
back half of this post, and it is the reason the setup ends up feeling good
rather than merely private.

## What you end up with

Clip the mic on, tap its button, talk, tap again. Text appears where your cursor
is, or a full meeting transcript with a summary lands in the app. Your hands
never go near the keyboard, nothing joins your call, and the audio stays on the
machine.

## What you need

| You need | Why |
| --- | --- |
| An Apple Silicon Mac | MacParakeet needs M1 or newer and macOS 14.2 or later. The transcription model runs on the neural engine. |
| A DJI wireless mic | Any of them work as a microphone. The button trick is confirmed on the 1st generation (`AST01` transmitter, `ASR01` receiver) and the Mic Mini 2, and step 7 tells you about yours in ten seconds. |
| The USB-C cable from the DJI box | It has to carry data. A charge-only cable is the single most common reason the receiver never shows up. |
| [Karabiner-Elements](https://karabiner-elements.pqrs.org) 15.3.18 or newer | Only for the button. The `is_consumer` device identifier landed in that version and the rule does not work without it. |
| Homebrew | For the one install command. |

## Part one: the mic

### Set the receiver to Mono

Do this before anything else, because it is the mistake that is hardest to spot
later.

In **Stereo** mode the DJI receiver puts transmitter 1 on the left channel and
transmitter 2 on the right. With a single mic that means half of every recording
is silence. It sounds like audio "in one ear", and it gives a transcriber less
to work with than it should have.

**Mono** mixes everything into one channel, which is what you want for speech.

- **Mic 2 and Mic 3**: on the receiver's touchscreen, swipe down or tap the gear,
  then Recording Mode, then Mono.
- **Mic Mini**: no screen, so set it from the DJI Mimo app on your phone.

Skip Safety Track. It records a second quieter copy as insurance for video
editing, which does nothing for you here.

### Plug it in and select it

Connect the receiver to your Mac with the USB-C cable, then open **System
Settings**, **Sound**, **Input**. A new device appears, usually called
`Wireless Microphone RX`. Select it.

Do not run the receiver's 3.5 mm jack into the Mac instead. A MacBook's
headphone port expects a headset-style plug and you will get poor audio or none.

### Set the gain on the receiver, not in macOS

Still in that Sound panel, talk normally and watch the input meter. Aim for it
bouncing around two thirds of the way across. Too quiet and you are transcribing
room noise along with speech; loud enough to peg the meter and the distortion
will produce nonsense words that no amount of AI cleanup fixes.

Adjust this on the DJI receiver itself. You get a better signal than turning a
quiet recording up afterwards.

## Part two: local transcription

### Install MacParakeet

```bash
brew install --cask macparakeet
```

It is free and open source under GPL-3.0. The transcription runs on NVIDIA's
Parakeet model, converted to run on Apple Silicon, so it is genuinely local
rather than a wrapper around somebody's API.

### First launch

Open it. macOS may note that it was downloaded from the internet, which is a
single click because the app is notarized.

It then downloads roughly 600 MB, the speech model plus speaker detection. This
happens once. After that, transcription works with no network at all.

### Grant the permissions it actually needs

| Permission | For | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microphone | Hearing the DJI mic | Always |
| Accessibility | Global hotkey and pasting text | Needed for the button trick |
| Screen Recording | Capturing the other person's voice on a call | Online meetings only |
| Calendar | Auto-starting on scheduled meetings | Optional |

<Callout type="note" title="Why a note taker wants Screen Recording">
  macOS has no separate "record system sound" permission. Capturing the far side
  of a Zoom or Meet call is gated behind Screen Recording, so an app that records
  meetings has to ask for it. If you only ever record in-person conversations,
  skip it: microphone-only mode never asks.
</Callout>

### Point it at the mic and pick a mode

In Settings, set the input to `Wireless Microphone RX`. If your version has no
per-device picker it follows the macOS system default, which you already set.

Then choose how much of the room you want:

- **Microphone only**, for in-person meetings and voice notes. No Screen
  Recording permission needed.
- **Microphone plus system audio**, for calls. Your voice comes from the DJI mic
  and everyone else's is pulled from your Mac's audio output. Nothing joins the
  call and nobody sees a recorder in the participant list.

### Summaries, if you want them

Transcription needs nothing else. Turning a transcript into notes needs a model,
and you have two honest options.

Point it at a cloud model (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter) for good
summaries. Only the transcript text is sent, never the audio, and an hour-long
meeting is on the order of 10 to 15 thousand tokens, so it costs pennies.

Or run it fully locally through Ollama or LM Studio. Summaries are noticeably
weaker, but nothing leaves the machine at all. On 16 GB of RAM, stay around 8B
models.

At this point the setup works. Everything below is about not having to touch the
laptop.

## Part three: the button

DJI documents the transmitter's link button as a camera shutter remote for
phones, and says nothing about computers. The mechanism gives the trick away
though: phone camera shutters fire on a volume keypress, so a shutter remote is
really just a device that sends volume events. Plug the receiver into a Mac and
those arrive as ordinary consumer HID.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check your mic actually sends something">
    With the receiver plugged in and the transmitter on and linked, tap the link
    button once.

    <VolumeHudSketch />

    If the volume HUD appears, everything below will work. If nothing happens,
    open Karabiner-EventViewer, go to the Devices tab, and look for the receiver.
    No entry there means it presents itself as an audio device only and no amount
    of configuration will help.

    <Callout type="warning" title="Tap, do not hold">
      Holding the link button starts pairing mode. The transmitter's green LED
      goes from steady to blinking, and if it completes you have to re-link the
      transmitter and receiver before the mic works again. Every test here is a
      short tap.
    </Callout>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Find the receiver's device identifiers">
    Karabiner registers every HID device it sees, so the identifiers are already
    in its config. Read them out rather than guessing:

    <Terminal>{`$ python3 -c "
import json, os
d = json.load(open(os.path.expanduser('~/.config/karabiner/karabiner.json')))
for dev in d['profiles'][0].get('devices', []):
    print(dev.get('identifiers'), 'ignore=', dev.get('ignore'))
"
{'is_consumer': True, 'product_id': 16392, 'vendor_id': 11427} ignore= False`}</Terminal>

    Vendor `11427` is DJI. The product id varies: `16392` is the 1st generation
    receiver and the Mic Mini 2 reports `16401`. Use whatever yours prints.

    `ignore= False` means Karabiner is already listening to the device, so there
    is nothing to enable first.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Map the event to F13">
    Open `karabiner.json`, in `~/.config/karabiner/`, and find the entry for your
    receiver under `profiles[0].devices`. Add a `simple_modifications` array:

    ```json
    {
      "identifiers": { "vendor_id": 11427, "product_id": 16392, "is_consumer": true },
      "ignore": false,
      "simple_modifications": [
        { "from": { "consumer_key_code": "volume_increment" }, "to": [{ "key_code": "f13" }] },
        { "from": { "consumer_key_code": "volume_decrement" }, "to": [{ "key_code": "f13" }] }
      ]
    }
    ```

    Both directions are mapped in case your receiver alternates between them
    across presses. Mine only ever sent `volume_increment`, but the second line
    costs nothing.

    The GUI does the same thing: the Simple Modifications tab has a target device
    dropdown at the top, and picking the receiver there writes this structure.

    <KarabinerDeviceSketch />

    Karabiner reloads the file the moment it changes, so there is nothing to
    restart. **The test: tap the button and the volume HUD no longer appears.**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Bind F13 in MacParakeet">
    Settings, Capture, Dictation. There are two hotkey rows and only one of them
    can work with this mic.

    <DictationHotkeySketch />

    Click **Change** on the hands-free row, then tap the link button. It records
    as F13.

    There is a small joke in this. A MacBook's function row stops at F12, so
    there is no way to type F13 on the built-in keyboard, which makes the DJI
    button the only device you own that can enter it into a hotkey field. The
    thing you are binding is also the only tool that can do the binding.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify it saved as a real key">
    Worth checking rather than trusting the label. MacParakeet stores the hotkey
    as JSON in a binary blob, so a plain `defaults read` shows you hex:

    <Terminal>{`$ defaults export com.macparakeet.MacParakeet - | grep -A2 hotkeyTrigger
{"kind":"keyCode","keyCode":105}
# 105 is F13. A "kind":"modifier" value here means it silently stayed on Fn.`}</Terminal>

    Then go and talk at something. Open any text field, tap the button, say a
    sentence, tap again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why not just map it to Fn

This was my first plan, and it is the obvious one, because double-tapping Fn is
already the system dictation gesture on a Mac.

It does not work, and the reason is worth knowing even if you never touch a DJI
mic. **Any Karabiner rule that touches Fn swallows the native double-tap.** The
Globe key is handled far enough down the stack that a synthetic press cannot
reproduce the gesture, so remapping Fn at all breaks double-tap dictation, and
mapping something else *to* Fn hits the same wall from the other side.

If you already have a rule involving Fn in your config, system dictation is
quietly broken on your machine right now and this is why.

F13 sidesteps it completely. No physical key sends it, so nothing competes for
it, and it needs no changes to any config you already have. A modifier
combination like `fn+space` is also fine, since the problem is specifically the
bare double-tap.

## Why hold-to-talk cannot work

The natural wish is push-to-talk: hold the button while you speak, release to
stop. It is not possible with this hardware, and it is worth saying so before
you spend an evening on it.

The link button has exactly two behaviours. A tap sends the shutter event. A
hold enters pairing mode. There is no sustained keypress anywhere in there, so
there is nothing for Karabiner to hold down.

The substitute is auto-stop after silence, which MacParakeet and most others
have. Tap once, say your piece, stop talking, and it ends by itself. Same
"do not touch anything to finish" feel, without holding anything.

## If you generate your karabiner.json

Skip this unless you use a tool that writes `karabiner.json` for you, such as
[karabiner-human-config](https://github.com/nrjdalal/karabiner-human-config),
which compiles a friendlier DSL down to the real thing.

There are two traps, and they are why this post puts the rule in `devices[]`
rather than the more obvious place.

**A complex modification would be wiped.** These generators typically rebuild
`profiles[0].complex_modifications.rules` from scratch on every run. I tested it
against a throwaway config first: `devices[]` and `global` were spread through
untouched, and a hand-written complex rule was gone. A per-device simple
modification lives in the part that survives.

**The DSL probably cannot express it anyway.** Karabiner needs
`consumer_key_code` rather than `key_code` in the `from` position for a volume
event, and a DSL that builds `{ key_code }` unconditionally has no way to emit
the other field. Worth reading your generator's source before fighting its
syntax.

## When it breaks

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| The receiver never appears in Sound, Input | Charge-only USB-C cable. Swap it, then try the other port, then power-cycle the receiver while plugged in. |
| Audio only in one ear, or half the waveform flat | The receiver is in Stereo. Set it to Mono. |
| The transcript is full of nonsense words | Gain too high and distorting, or too low and picking up the noise floor. Reset to the two-thirds target. |
| Only your voice in the transcript, not theirs | You are in microphone-only mode. Switch to microphone plus system audio and grant Screen Recording. |
| Only their voice, not yours | macOS flipped back to the built-in mic, or the call app grabbed the DJI exclusively. Re-select the receiver, and set it inside the call app too. |
| The volume HUD is back | Karabiner stopped intercepting. Check the device has **Modify events** ticked, and try `ignore_vendor_events: false` on its entry. |
| The button does nothing at all | The transmitter unpaired, almost always from holding the link button rather than tapping. Hold it on both units to re-link. |
| It worked, then stopped after a config regen | The rule was in `complex_modifications`. Move it to `devices[]`. |
| The hotkey quietly reverted to Fn | Re-check with the `defaults export` command above. The settings label is not proof. |
| Everything looks right and no text appears | Accessibility permission. A global hotkey does not fire without it. |
| The mic cuts out mid-meeting | Transmitter battery. They run about five hours. Charge in the case between meetings. |

## Worth knowing

None of this needed a driver, a companion app, or anything DJI ships. The
receiver was already presenting itself as a keyboard, and the entire job was
noticing that and redirecting one keypress.

That shape is more common than you would think. Plenty of USB peripherals send
standard HID that has nothing to do with their advertised purpose, and
Karabiner-EventViewer will show you what any of them are actually saying. A
device you already own may have a button on it that macOS is willing to listen
to.
