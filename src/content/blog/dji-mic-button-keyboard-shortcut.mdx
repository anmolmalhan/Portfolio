Dictation software has a shortcut problem. The trigger has to be a key you never
press by accident, which means it ends up somewhere awkward, and you still have
to reach for the keyboard to start talking. That is a strange requirement for a
feature whose whole point is that you are not typing.

Meanwhile there is already a button clipped to your shirt.

DJI wireless mics have a link button on the transmitter. Hold it and the
transmitter pairs with the receiver. Tap it and it acts as a camera shutter
remote for a phone. DJI documents that second behaviour for phones only, and
says nothing about computers, but the mechanism gives the trick away: phone
camera shutters fire on a volume keypress. A shutter remote is really just a
device that sends volume events.

Plug the receiver into a Mac over USB-C and those same events arrive as ordinary
consumer HID. Anything that can see a keyboard can see the button.

## What this gets you

Tap the mic on your shirt, talk, tap it again, and the text lands wherever your
cursor is. Your hands never go near the laptop, and nothing about it is specific
to one dictation app. You are producing a keypress, so anything that takes a
global hotkey will take it.

I use it with [MacParakeet](https://macparakeet.com), which transcribes locally
on Apple Silicon, but Wispr Flow, macOS Dictation and anything else with a
configurable shortcut work the same way.

## Before you start

| You need | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A DJI wireless mic | Confirmed on the 1st generation (`AST01` transmitter, `ASR01` receiver) and on the Mic Mini 2. Other models very likely work, and step one tells you in about ten seconds. |
| [Karabiner-Elements](https://karabiner-elements.pqrs.org) 15.3.18 or newer | The `is_consumer` device identifier landed in that version, and the rule does not work without it. |
| A dictation app with a global hotkey | Any of them. This post ends at "a key gets pressed". |
| The USB-C cable from the DJI box | It has to carry data. A charge-only cable is the single most common reason the receiver never shows up. |

## First, check your mic actually sends something

Everything below depends on this, and it costs nothing to find out.

Plug the receiver into your Mac, make sure the transmitter is on and linked,
then tap the link button once.

**If the macOS volume HUD pops up, you are done here.** That popup is the proof.
The button is already talking to your Mac, it is just talking about volume, and
the rest of this post is redirecting what it says.

<VolumeHudSketch />

If nothing happens at all, open Karabiner-EventViewer, go to the Devices tab,
and look for the receiver. No entry there means it presents itself as an audio
device only, with no HID interface, and no amount of configuration will help.

<Callout type="warning" title="Tap, do not hold">
  Holding the link button starts pairing mode. You will see the transmitter's
  green LED change from steady to blinking, and if it completes you have to
  re-link the transmitter and receiver before the mic works again. Every test in
  this post is a short tap.
</Callout>

## The setup

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the receiver's device identifiers">
    Karabiner registers every HID device it sees, so once the receiver has been
    plugged in the identifiers are already sitting in its config. Read them out
    rather than guessing:

    <Terminal>{`$ python3 -c "
import json, os
d = json.load(open(os.path.expanduser('~/.config/karabiner/karabiner.json')))
for dev in d['profiles'][0].get('devices', []):
    print(dev.get('identifiers'), 'ignore=', dev.get('ignore'))
"
{'is_consumer': True, 'product_id': 16392, 'vendor_id': 11427} ignore= False`}</Terminal>

    Vendor `11427` is DJI. The product id is the part that varies: `16392` is the
    1st generation receiver, and the Mic Mini 2 reports `16401`. Use whatever
    yours prints.

    `ignore= False` is worth noticing. It means Karabiner is already listening to
    the device and you do not have to enable anything first.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Map the event to F13">
    Open `karabiner.json`, in `~/.config/karabiner/`, and find the entry for
    your receiver under `profiles[0].devices`. Add a `simple_modifications`
    array to it:

    ```json
    {
      "identifiers": { "vendor_id": 11427, "product_id": 16392, "is_consumer": true },
      "ignore": false,
      "simple_modifications": [
        { "from": { "consumer_key_code": "volume_increment" }, "to": [{ "key_code": "f13" }] },
        { "from": { "consumer_key_code": "volume_decrement" }, "to": [{ "key_code": "f13" }] }
      ]
    }
    ```

    Both directions are mapped in case your receiver alternates between them
    across presses. Mine only ever sent `volume_increment`, but the second line
    costs nothing.

    You can do exactly the same thing through the GUI if you prefer: the Simple
    Modifications tab has a target device dropdown at the top, and picking the
    receiver there writes this same structure.

    <KarabinerDeviceSketch />

    Karabiner reloads the file the moment it changes, so there is nothing to
    restart. **The test: tap the button and the volume HUD no longer appears.**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Bind F13 in your dictation app">
    Open your app's hotkey setting, click into the shortcut field, and tap the
    link button. It records as F13.

    There is a small joke in this. A MacBook's function row stops at F12, so
    there is no way to type F13 on the built-in keyboard, which makes the DJI
    button the only device you own that can enter it into a hotkey field. The
    thing you are binding is also the only tool that can do the binding.

    Pick the toggle-style trigger if your app offers a choice, the one where you
    tap to start and tap again to stop. The next section explains why the
    hold-to-talk variant is not an option.

    <DictationHotkeySketch />
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify it saved as a real key">
    Worth doing explicitly rather than trusting the label in the UI. Most apps
    store the hotkey as structured data, and for MacParakeet it is JSON kept as
    a binary blob, so a plain `defaults read` shows you hex:

    <Terminal>{`$ defaults export com.macparakeet.MacParakeet - | grep -A2 hotkeyTrigger
{"kind":"keyCode","keyCode":105}
# 105 is F13. A "kind":"modifier" value here means it silently stayed on Fn.`}</Terminal>

    Then go and talk at something. Open any text field, tap the button, say a
    sentence, tap again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why not just map it to Fn

This was my first plan, and it is the obvious one, because double-tapping Fn is
already the system dictation gesture on a Mac.

It does not work, and the reason is worth knowing even if you never touch a DJI
mic. **Any Karabiner rule that touches Fn swallows the native double-tap.** The
Globe key is handled far enough down the stack that a synthetic press cannot
reproduce the gesture, so remapping Fn at all breaks double-tap dictation, and
mapping something else *to* Fn hits the same wall from the other side.

If you already have a rule involving Fn in your config, system dictation is
quietly broken on your machine right now and this is why.

F13 sidesteps it completely. No physical key on the keyboard sends it, so
nothing competes for it, and it needs no changes to any config you already have.
A modifier combination like `fn+space` is also fine, since the problem is
specifically the bare double-tap.

## Why hold-to-talk cannot work

The natural ergonomic wish is push-to-talk: hold the button while you speak,
release to stop. It is not possible with this hardware, and it is worth saying
so plainly before you spend an evening on it.

The link button has exactly two behaviours. A tap sends the shutter event. A
hold enters pairing mode. There is no sustained keypress state anywhere in
there, so there is nothing for Karabiner to hold down. This is a property of the
button, not of your configuration.

The good substitute is an auto-stop-after-silence setting, which most dictation
apps have. Tap once, say your piece, stop talking, and it ends by itself. You
get the same "do not touch anything to finish" feel without holding anything.

## If you generate your karabiner.json

Skip this unless you use a tool that writes `karabiner.json` for you, such as
[karabiner-human-config](https://github.com/nrjdalal/karabiner-human-config),
which compiles a friendlier DSL down to the real thing.

There are two traps, and they are the reason this post puts the rule in
`devices[]` rather than the more obvious place.

**A complex modification would be wiped.** These generators typically rebuild
`profiles[0].complex_modifications.rules` from scratch on every run. I tested it
against a throwaway config before touching anything real: `devices[]` and
`global` were spread through untouched, and a hand-written complex rule was
gone. A per-device simple modification lives in the part that survives, so
regenerating your config does not cost you the mapping.

**The DSL probably cannot express it anyway.** Karabiner needs
`consumer_key_code` rather than `key_code` in the `from` position for a volume
event, and a DSL that builds `{ key_code }` unconditionally has no way to emit
the other field. Worth a look at your generator's source before you spend time
fighting its syntax.

<Callout type="tip">
  Both problems disappear at once by using a per-device simple modification. It
  is also the only version of this rule you can set entirely through the
  Karabiner GUI, which makes it the easier path even if you write JSON by hand
  for everything else.
</Callout>

## When it breaks

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| The volume HUD is back | Karabiner stopped intercepting. Check that the device has **Modify events** ticked in the Devices tab, and try setting `ignore_vendor_events` to `false` on its entry. |
| The button does nothing at all | The transmitter unpaired, almost always from holding the link button rather than tapping it. Hold it on both units to re-link. |
| It worked, then stopped after you regenerated your config | The rule was in `complex_modifications`. Move it to `devices[]`. |
| The hotkey quietly reverted to Fn | Re-check with the `defaults export` command above. The label in the settings window is not proof. |
| Everything looks right and no text appears | Grant the dictation app Accessibility permission. A global hotkey does not fire without it. |

## Worth knowing

None of this needed a driver, a companion app, or anything DJI ships. The
receiver was already presenting itself as a keyboard, and the entire job was
noticing that and redirecting one keypress.

That is a fairly common shape once you go looking. Plenty of USB peripherals
send standard HID that has nothing to do with their advertised purpose, and
Karabiner-EventViewer will show you what any of them are actually saying. A
device you already own may have a button on it that macOS is willing to listen
to.
